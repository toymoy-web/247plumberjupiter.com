High Water Bill in Jupiter, FL: How to Find the Cause

A jump in the bill usually means a leak or a change in use. A short meter test and a few checks tell you which, and where to look next.

By the Jupiter 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 3, 2026 · Updated October 3, 2026

    Short Answer

    The Fastest Way to Find the Cause

    Close every tap and appliance, find your meter, and watch the low-flow indicator for at least 15 minutes. If it keeps moving, water is escaping somewhere on your property. If it holds still, the extra usage is coming from fixtures, irrigation, or a change in how the house is used.

    The next step is to separate inside from outside. Close the main shutoff at the house and look again. If the meter still moves, the leak is between the meter and the house. If it stops, the leak is inside.

    Local Note

    Jupiter Note: Tequesta Has a Different Water Utility

    Not every Jupiter-area address is billed by the Town. The Village of Tequesta runs its own Water Department, which also serves Jupiter Inlet Colony. If you live in Tequesta, a high bill is a question for the Village, not the Town of Jupiter.

    In Juno Beach, some addresses are served by Seacoast Utility Authority. Check the name at the top of your bill before you call anyone.

    Source: Village of Tequesta Water Department and South Florida Water Management District water-supply plan.

    Your Bills

    Two Bills, Two Agencies

    In Jupiter, the water on your bill generally comes from the Town of Jupiter Utilities Department. Sewer service in Jupiter, Tequesta, and parts of Juno Beach and Jupiter Farms comes from the Loxahatchee River District, which bills quarterly. A higher water reading can therefore raise the sewer charge too, since the District uses water consumption as part of how it bills.

    For non-residential customers, the District calculates sewer bills from a 12-month rolling average of water use. Homes on private wells, as in much of Jupiter Farms, have no Town water bill at all. Their warning sign is a pump that cycles constantly or an electric bill that rises.

    Test

    A Step-by-Step Meter Test

    1

    Close everything

    Turn off taps, the ice maker, the washing machine, and irrigation timers.

    2

    Find the meter

    Lift the lid on the meter box near the street and find the leak indicator, often a small triangle or dial.

    3

    Watch it

    Watch for 15 minutes. Any movement means water is escaping.

    4

    Close the house main

    Shut the main valve at the house and watch again.

    5

    Note the result

    Still moving points outside the house. Stopped points inside.

    Toilets and Irrigation

    The Two Most Common Hidden Leaks

    A running toilet can waste 200 gallons or more a day. To test, add a few drops of food coloring to the tank, wait without flushing, and check the bowl. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking. The fix is usually inexpensive, and the toilet repair page explains the parts.

    Irrigation is the other big one in Florida. A cracked line or a stuck valve can run for weeks, especially in summer when the system runs more. A soggy patch, unusually green grass, or a pressure drop at the sprinklers all point to a leak.

    Town Meters

    What the Town's Meter Upgrade Means for You

    The Town of Jupiter's Utilities Department plans a system-wide upgrade to Advanced Metering Infrastructure, starting in spring 2026 and expected to finish in April 2028. According to the Town, the new meters will bring early leak detection, high-bill alerts, and near real-time usage data through an online portal and mobile app.

    The upgrade schedule decides if your neighborhood has new meters yet. If it has, the portal can show when water was used, and a steady overnight flow is a strong sign of a leak. If not, the meter test still works.

    Reading the Meter

    How to Read the Numbers

    A water meter keeps a running total, much like a car odometer. Write down the reading at the same time each day for a few days, and subtract each from the next to see daily use. If you know nothing was running overnight, the overnight difference should be near zero.

    Compare your readings with the bill. If the meter total is higher than the last bill's reading and your habits have not changed, you are using more than before, and the next step is to find where.

    Seasonal Patterns

    Florida Patterns That Move the Bill

    In the dry months irrigation often runs more, and a pool that loses water to heat and wind adds to the draw. In summer, rain can cover a leak because lawns stay green without watering. Seasonal residents and guests change household use too, and a house that is empty for weeks should show almost no usage.

    That last point is a useful test. If the house has been vacant and the meter still moves, water is escaping. A vacant house with a normal-looking bill is a stronger leak signal than an occupied one.

    A Technician

    What a Technician Adds

    A plumber can narrow the leak without opening walls. Pressure tests on isolated lines show whether the hot side or the cold side is losing water, and listening and thermal tools pinpoint the spot. That saves time and avoids cutting in the wrong place.

    If the leak is under the slab, the under-slab leak repair page explains the options. A leaking irrigation line or hose bib is usually a smaller repair, and a failing faucet is covered under faucet and sink repair.

    Next Steps

    When It Is a Leak

    If the leak is inside, leak tracing traces it before any wall is opened. If it is between the meter and the house, water line repair covers the service line. Ask the Town's customer service whether any billing adjustment applies after a repair, since policies differ.

    In Juno Beach, FL 33408, some addresses are served by Seacoast Utility Authority instead of the Town, so check your bill to see which utility to ask.
