I Got a Notice That My Backflow Device Is Due

The Town of Jupiter sends a notice when a backflow prevention device is due for testing. The notice often comes as a surprise to homeowners with an irrigation system, because the device sits out of sight beside the house or near the meter.

The fix is simple. Schedule a certified tester, have the device checked, and make sure the result reaches the Town. This page explains what is involved.

Overview

What Backflow Prevention Is

Backflow is water flowing in the wrong direction, from a private system back into the public supply. Irrigation lines, fire sprinklers, boilers, and commercial equipment can contain fertilizer, chemicals, or stagnant water that must never reach the drinking water.

A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that stops that reverse flow. It has internal springs and seals that wear, which is why the device is tested on a schedule and repaired when it fails.

Safety

What to Do If You Suspect Contaminated Water

Do not drink it

If water looks, smells, or tastes wrong after a device failure, stop drinking and cooking with it.

Close irrigation

Shut off the irrigation system and any connected equipment until the device is tested.

Avoid cross-connections

Never leave a hose submerged in a pool, bucket, or chemical container.

Call for a test

A certified tester can check the device and report the result to the Town.

Jupiter Rules

How the Town of Jupiter Handles Backflow

The Town's Utilities Department explains that only licensed plumbing contractors may install backflow prevention devices, and that testing must be done by a Certified Backflow Prevention Technician. The tester uploads the results directly to the Town's database, so there is no paper to mail.

Not every property needs a device. The Town's design standards set out which connections require one, and irrigation connections and many commercial properties commonly do.

If you need extra time after a notice, the Town asks that you contact its backflow program to coordinate. Waiting past the due date without contact is the common mistake.

Process

How a Test Visit Works

1

Locate

The device is found and its size, type, and serial number are noted.

2

Test

A calibrated test kit measures check valves and relief valve operation.

3

Repair if needed

Failed seals or springs are replaced and the device is retested.

4

Report

The result is uploaded to the Town's database.

5

Record

You get a copy of the result for your files.

Decision

Repair or Replace a Failed Device

Repair when

  • Only a seal or spring has failed
  • The body is not corroded
  • The device is accessible
  • Parts are available

Replace when

  • The body is cracked or heavily corroded
  • The device is obsolete
  • The wrong type was installed
  • Repairs cost close to a new unit
