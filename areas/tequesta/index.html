Housing Era

A 1957 Country Club Village and Its Pipes

Tequesta was planned around a golf course. Developer Charles Martyn laid out roughly 600 residential plots for the Tequesta Country Club, and the village incorporated in 1957. The first golf course opened a year later, so the oldest homes here are now close to seventy years old.

Houses from that era often still have original drain lines under the slab, galvanized or early copper supply pipe, and sewer laterals that have been working for decades. None of that means a failure is coming, but it explains why a backup or a pinhole leak in 33469 so often turns out to be an old-pipe story.

Emergency dispatch for Tequesta runs around the clock, and the first call usually sorts the problem into one of three buckets: water coming in, water not going out, or water not coming on.

Utilities

Water, Sewer, and Who to Call

Tequesta operates its own Water Utilities Department from 345 Tequesta Drive, which is unusual for a village its size. Wastewater is treated by the Loxahatchee River District. That makes two agencies on the public side and a plumber on the private side.

A simple test helps at 2 a.m. If the water is out on the whole street, call the village. If the meter is fine but a fixture is dripping, a drain is backing up, or a ceiling is wet, the problem is inside the property line and the 24/7 line is the right call.

A call to the 24/7 line in Tequesta reaches dispatch, not a voicemail box. Give the street, say whether the property is a house or a condo, and describe what you can see: water moving, a drain backing up, or no water at all.

Condos

Riverfront Condos and Shared Risers

Tequesta mixes single-family homes with condominiums on the Loxahatchee River and the Intracoastal Waterway. In a condo, a leak can cross unit lines through shared drain stacks and supply risers, so the first step is different from a house.

Call building management as soon as water is moving, because the association may control the shutoff for a whole riser. Then call the plumbing line so the source is confirmed before anyone opens a wall. Photograph the damage in your unit for the association and your own insurer.

Repairs to shared stacks and risers in a multi-unit building fall under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), so the association may require a permit even when the fix looks small.

Tequesta Country Club, Constitution Park, and the Colony Next Door

The village grew around Tequesta Country Club, and its streets of 1950s homes sit within a short drive of Constitution Park and Tequesta Park, also called Kiwanis Recreation Park. The 41-acre Tequesta Park was established in 1956 and is the site of the village's water wells.
