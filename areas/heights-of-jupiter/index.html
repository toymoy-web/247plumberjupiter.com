The Area

A Neighborhood With Two Names

The Heights of Jupiter is the name residents use for a subdivision whose legal name is North Palm Beach Heights. It lies north of Donald Ross Road, west of Abacoa, south of Egret Landing and east of Interstate 95. The shorter name avoids confusion with the Village of North Palm Beach.

A developer bought the land in 1956 and the state created a water control district in 1958, but the first homes were not built until the early 1980s. The area waited for city water and sewer, and in 1984 bonds funded both, along with road paving.

Built After the Pipes

Homes That Came After the Water and Sewer Lines

Most homes in the Heights were built after the water and sewer systems went in, so the neighborhood runs on public sewer rather than septic. That is a real difference from some other parts of the area, and it means a backed-up drain is a question of where the blockage sits.

Real estate summaries describe most Heights homes as having no homeowners association. Without an association, the owner decides on repairs, and there is no manager to call about a shared problem.

Who Handles What

Street Side and House Side

In general the utility looks after its pipes in the street and the owner looks after the line on the property side. Where that dividing line falls depends on the meter and cleanout locations, so confirm with the utility if you are not sure.

A simple check helps. If neighbors on your street also have no water or low pressure, call the utility. If only your house is affected, the problem is on your side, and a plumber is the right call.

Meter Check

A Quick Way to Spot a Hidden Leak

1

Close everything

Turn off taps and appliances that use water.

2

Find the meter

Lift the lid on the meter box near the street.

3

Watch the dial

If the leak indicator keeps moving for fifteen minutes, water is escaping.

4

Call for help

Moving means a leak somewhere on the property, and leak detection can locate it.
