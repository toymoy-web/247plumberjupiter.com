Water Heater Permit in Jupiter, FL: Do You Need One?

Short answer: yes. The Town of Jupiter requires a permit to replace a water heater, and the office changes once you cross a county line.

    Short Answer

    Yes, and Here Is Who Issues It

    Inside the Town of Jupiter, replacing a water heater requires a permit from the Town Building Department at 210 Military Trail. The Town's water heater replacement form asks for the tank size, fuel, whether the unit is hard wired or cord and plug, and the old and new heater wattage and breaker size. It also states that changing from a tank to a tankless unit requires plan review.

    The permit exists so an inspector can confirm the install is safe: correct venting or electrical connection, a relief valve, and a drain. A like-for-like swap is the simplest case and the Town's form says its goal is to close these permits at the first inspection.

    Who Pulls It

    Contractor or Owner

    Florida Statutes Chapter 489 requires construction work to be done by a licensed contractor. The Town of Jupiter's own permit paperwork notes that an owner-builder application exists for homeowners who do the work themselves, with disclosures about the risks of not hiring a licensed contractor. For most people, the contractor pulls the permit.

    Ask any plumber for the Florida license number and check it on the state's license search. Ask who pulls the permit and whether the permit fee is in the price.

    Code

    What the Inspector Looks For

    Water heater work follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Inspectors commonly check for a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe, a drain pan with its own drain where a leak would damage the building, proper venting on gas units, and a correctly sized breaker and wiring on electric units.

    A missing relief discharge pipe or an undersized breaker is the kind of thing that fails an inspection. Ask your plumber to point these out at the end of the job.

    Backfire

    Why Skipping the Permit Backfires

    An unpermitted water heater can surface during a home sale, an insurance inspection, or a claim. A buyer's inspector may ask for permit records, and an insurer may question work that was never inspected. Closing a permit later can mean opening walls or redoing work, which costs more than doing it right.

    A permit also gives you a record. An inspector looked at the relief valve, the drain, the venting, and the wiring, and that record is useful if something goes wrong.

    Special Cases

    Cases That Add Steps

    Changing from gas to electric, or from a tank to a tankless unit, adds work beyond a like-for-like swap. A tankless conversion can involve new venting, a larger gas line, or a bigger electrical circuit, and the Town requires plan review for it. Heat pump water heaters need a suitable space, a drain, and the right circuit.

    A unit in an attic or a tight closet needs a pan and a discharge path, and a condo may restrict where a heater can go. Mention these on the first call so the quote is realistic.

    Ask Your Plumber

    Questions to Ask Before You Hire

    Who pulls the permit?

    Ask for the contractor's Florida license number and the permit number.

    Is the fee in the price?

    Permit and inspection fees should be stated up front.

    What is included?

    Ask about the pan, the discharge pipe, the relief valve, and haul-away.

    What is the warranty?

    Ask how long parts and labor are covered.

    Timing

    Planning Around the Permit

    If your heater fails on a Friday night, the permit does not stop a plumber from restoring hot water. The permit and inspection follow the install. Ask the contractor how they handle that sequence, and how soon the inspection is scheduled.

    If you are planning a replacement before the old tank fails, do it on your own schedule and ask about plan review early if you want to convert to tankless.

    Caution

    Confirm Before You Start

    Permit rules, fees, and forms change. This page describes the Town's published form and the county pages as of this writing. Call or check the building department's site to confirm current requirements for your address.
