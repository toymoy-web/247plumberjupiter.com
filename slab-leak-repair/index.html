Warm Floor or Running Water With Nothing On?

Most calls about this problem start with something odd rather than a name. A patch of tile that feels warm underfoot, a water meter that spins with every tap closed, or a faint hiss at night all point to the same cause: a pressurized pipe leaking under the concrete.

The good news is that the leak can be located without breaking up the whole floor. The job is to find the exact spot, then choose between a small repair and a larger fix.

Emergency

Emergency Slab Leak Signs: Water at the Floor or a Running Meter

Treat it as an emergency when water is seeping up through the concrete, a floor feels warm underfoot, or the meter keeps turning with every fixture off. The water bill that rises for weeks is the slower version of the same problem.

Close the main shutoff to slow the loss and call the 24/7 line. Tell dispatch whether the damp spot is in a bedroom, the garage edge, or near the water heater, since that helps narrow which line to test first.

Overview

What a Slab Leak Is

A slab leak is a leak in a water supply or drain pipe that runs under or inside a concrete slab foundation. Slab-on-grade construction is the norm across South Florida, so supply lines often run through or beneath the concrete instead of through an open crawlspace.

Hot-water lines are common culprits because heat and expansion stress the pipe, and the warm spot on a floor is the classic sign. Cold-water leaks are harder to feel and usually show up first as a bill or a meter reading.

Repair Options

Three Ways to Fix a Line Under the Slab

The right fix depends on how many leaks there are, the pipe material, and how much of the floor you are willing to open.

OptionWhat it involvesTrade-off
Spot repairOpen the slab at the pinpointed leak, cut out the bad section, and replace itQuickest, but another leak may follow if the pipe is aging
RerouteAbandon the leaking run and install a new line through the attic or wallsAvoids concrete work, but opens walls and ceilings
RepipeReplace the whole supply system, often with PEXHighest upfront cost, best when leaks keep returning

Jupiter Conditions

Why Slab Leaks Show Up in Jupiter Houses

Jupiter grew from 3,136 residents in 1970 to 24,986 in 1990, per U.S. Census counts, so a large share of its older houses were built in those two decades. Copper supply lines in that period can develop pinhole leaks with age, and polybutylene pipe, widely installed nationally from about 1978 to 1995, can fail at its fittings.

Permits matter on the bigger fixes. The Town of Jupiter Building Department at 210 Military Trail handles permits inside town limits, and Palm Beach County covers unincorporated areas such as Jupiter Farms. The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), governs the work. Florida Statutes Chapter 489 requires construction work to be done by a licensed contractor, so ask who pulls the permit before you sign.

Cost and permits are easier to plan when the leak is found early. A slow leak here can undermine soil under the slab and run up a water bill for months before the first stain appears.

Prep

If You Suspect a Slab Leak

Close the main

Shutting the main valve stops the loss while you wait.

Switch off the heater

If a hot-water line is involved, turn off power or gas to the water heater so it does not run dry.

Note the spots

Mark warm tiles with painter's tape so the technician can see where you felt heat.

Check your policy

Read the water damage and seepage terms before deciding how much of the floor to open.

Under the Home

Slab, Crawlspace, or Raised Home: Where the Pipes Run

Most Jupiter homes are slab on grade, so supply and drain lines run in or under the concrete. Some older or raised homes have a crawlspace instead, which makes the lines easier to reach but not easier to find a leak in. The pressure test works the same either way.

If a plumber has already looked and could not find it, ask that the isolated line test and a thermal scan be done together. The two methods catch different leaks.

Around Jupiter

Where Slab Leaks Turn Up: Indian Creek, Jonathan's Landing, and Beyond

Slab-on-grade construction is the norm across South Florida, and Jupiter is no exception. Indian Creek alone holds 12 subdivisions, and Jonathan's Landing opened its first villages in 1979. Houses from those decades have had supply lines under the concrete for thirty to forty years.

A warm patch on tile in any of these communities is worth a call the same day. Slab leaks near the river or the Intracoastal can also soak the soil before they reach the surface, so the pinpoint comes before any cutting.

Related Work

Before and After the Repair

If you only suspect a leak and want to confirm it first, start with leak location work. A leak that has already soaked the floor may also need drying and restoration to dry the subfloor and walls. A failed line is also covered under split supply line repair when the pipe has fully let go. If leaks keep returning, whole-home repiping replaces the supply system.
