Your Restroom Is Out or the Kitchen Drain Is Backing Up

A business cannot wait a week for a plumber. A dead restroom, a backed-up kitchen drain, or a leak above a ceiling means lost customers and sometimes a health inspection. The first step is a clear description over the phone so dispatch knows which emergency it is.

Tell the dispatcher the type of business, the fixture or area, and whether water is spreading. That decides who is sent and what equipment comes with them.

Emergency

Emergency Commercial Plumbing: Floor Drains, Restrooms, and Leaks

In a business, the emergency is usually a floor drain overflowing, a restroom flooding, a failed water heater, or water appearing in a parking lot or common area. Close the nearest shutoff, keep customers out of the wet area, and call the 24/7 line.

Tell dispatch if the building is owned or rented. Landlord and tenant responsibilities differ, and the building manager may control the main valve.

Overview

What Commercial Plumbing Covers

Commercial plumbing covers the water, drain, and gas systems in businesses and multi-unit properties. That includes restroom fixtures, floor drains, grease interceptors, water heaters and boilers, hose bibbs, backflow assemblies, and the lines feeding them.

Buildings differ from houses in scale and in rules. Commercial work follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Larger pipes, more fixtures per line, and higher stakes for failures mean a different approach to diagnosis and scheduling.

Safety

What to Do Before a Plumber Arrives

Close the shutoff

Know where the building's main and any fixture shutoffs are, and close them if water is spreading.

Protect people

Keep customers and staff away from wet floors and from any backed-up sewage.

Cut power near water

Do not touch wet electrical equipment. Turn off the breaker only if you can do so safely.

Document

Photograph the area and the damage for your insurer and your landlord.

Jupiter Conditions

Business Districts and What They Run Into

Jupiter's commercial areas range from the Abacoa town center and its stadium to the offices, labs, and clinics around Jupiter Park Drive and the Florida Atlantic University campus. Restaurants cluster along Indiantown Road, US 1, and PGA Boulevard across the line in Palm Beach Gardens.

The Loxahatchee River District treats wastewater for most of the Jupiter area and sets the rules for what can go down a commercial drain. Restaurants and similar businesses should ask the District about grease interceptor requirements and pump-out schedules.

Commercial customers also face the Town's backflow program. The Town sends a notice when a device is due, and testing must be done by a Certified Backflow Prevention Technician with the result uploaded to the Town.

Leak Surveys

Commercial Leak Detection for Shops, Plazas, and Multi-Unit Buildings

Business and multi-unit calls often start with a utility notice of continuous water use or a damp patch in a parking lot or a shared wall. Buildings with several units make it harder to tell which line is losing water, so isolating sections one at a time comes first.

Duplexes, triplexes, and small apartment buildings follow the same method as a shop. Property managers can describe the meter layout and which valves serve which units before the visit.

Grease and Floor Drains

Grease Traps, Floor Drains, and Grates

Restaurants and shops with floor drains and grease interceptors see backups when the trap is full or the line is blocked. Grease trap pumping is usually done by a licensed hauler, and plumbing support for the drain line can be arranged on the call.

A steel drain cover or grate that has to come off quickly is a common emergency request. Tell dispatch what kind of cover it is.
