Why Does My Water Taste or Smell Off?

Chlorine taste, a rotten-egg smell, cloudy water, or brown staining are common complaints. Each has a different cause and a different filter. Chlorine or chloramine taste points to carbon filtration. A rotten-egg smell is usually hydrogen sulfide, common in well water. Brown staining suggests iron.

Picking a filter before knowing which problem you have is the usual way people spend money and stay unhappy. The first step is a test.

Overview

What Water Filtration Covers

Filtration installation puts treatment equipment on the water line, at a single tap or for the whole house. Common systems include under-sink reverse osmosis for drinking water, whole-home carbon filters for chlorine and taste, sediment filters for particles, and specialized units for iron and sulfur on well water.

Performance claims should match published standards. NSF/ANSI 42 covers aesthetic effects such as taste and odor, NSF/ANSI 53 covers health-related contaminants, and NSF/ANSI 58 covers reverse osmosis systems. Ask for the certification on any product.

Jupiter Conditions

Town Water, Wells, and What to Ask

The Town of Jupiter treats its water through nanofiltration and reverse osmosis, performs thousands of tests a year, and publishes a yearly water quality report along with a PFAS statement and reference guide. Town customers can read those documents before buying any equipment.

Private wells in Jupiter Farms are not covered by that reporting. A homeowner is responsible for testing and treating the well, and listings across the area show owners using softeners, reverse osmosis drinking systems, and treatment for sulfur odor.

Whatever the source, a lab test is the best starting point. A plumber can take a sample or test hardness and chlorine on site, but contaminants such as nitrates or metals call for a certified lab.

Decision

Under-Sink or Whole-Home

Under-sink reverse osmosis

  • Improves drinking and cooking water
  • Small footprint and lower cost
  • One dedicated faucet
  • Wastes some water during filtering

Whole-home filter

  • Treats every tap and shower
  • Protects fixtures and appliances
  • Larger equipment on the main line
  • Needs periodic cartridge or media changes
