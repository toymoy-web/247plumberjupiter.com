The Village

A Planned Village Cut by Canals

August 13, 1956 is the incorporation date on file, and the plan came first. According to the village's 50th anniversary booklet, the original layout called for 20 canals, 3 lakes, and 75 miles of sewer pipe, which means a great deal of buried infrastructure under what was open land.

The first houses went up in the late 1950s, many bought by engineers from the Pratt & Whitney plant that opened in 1958, per the village booklet. Arthur Rutenberg built many of the early homes, according to the Palm Beach County history education site. Houses from that era are now seventy years old or close to it, and the pipes inside them have lived through several decades of repairs and remodels.

The 24/7 line reaches the village from Jupiter in roughly 17 minutes without traffic, about 9.6 road miles down US 1.

Warning Signs

Signs an Older Village Home Needs a Look

Water bill creeping up

A leak under a slab or in a yard line shows on the meter before it shows on a floor.

Hot water slow to arrive

A long wait can mean a failing heater or a line losing heat into the slab.

Green stains on a fixture

Mineral buildup around a valve means water has been weeping there for a while.

Drains slow in more than one room

A shared branch or the main line is the likelier cause than a single trap.

Soft or sunken lawn near the house

A wet patch between the meter and the wall can mean a service line leak.

Low pressure at every tap

A partly closed valve, a failing regulator, or a leak ahead of the house can all do it.

Emergency

Emergency Plumber North Palm Beach FL 33408: Canal House or Marina Condo

When a supply line lets go in the village, the first useful fact is the type of building. Tell dispatch if you are in a single-family house on a canal, a townhome, or a condo in a marina community such as Old Port Cove, Seamark, or Harbor Isles, all named in the village history booklet.

A house owner can usually close the main, cut power to the wet area, and wait for the crew. A condo owner often has a riser shutoff controlled by the building, so call the association's after-hours number at the same time you call the 24/7 line.

From 33408 or 33410, give the street and the nearest cross street. The village is ringed by water and reached over bridges, so the route a technician takes depends on which side of the water you are on.

Utilities

Seacoast Utility Authority and Your Side of the Meter

Seacoast Utility Authority lists the Village of North Palm Beach among the communities it serves, along with Palm Beach Gardens, Lake Park, and Juno Beach. The Authority supplies water, wastewater, and reclaimed water.

That split decides the first call in an emergency. A pressure loss that hits the whole street is a call to the utility. A leak after the meter is a plumbing call, and so is a drain that backs up inside the house.

Prep

Before We Arrive: If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak

Close the main

Turn the main shutoff to closed. In a condo, ask the building where the unit shutoff is.

Cut power to wet areas

Switch off breakers for outlets near standing water before anyone steps in.

Clear the area

Move rugs, electronics, and furniture out of the wet zone and open the doors to the room.

Call the 24/7 line

Give the street, the ZIP, and what you see so the right truck is loaded.

Services

Services That Fit 33408 and 33410 Properties

Older village homes lean on meter and pressure testing for lines that run under the slab, tank repair for tanks in garages and closets, and line clearing when a branch or main backs up.

A line that fails outright is an pipe failure repair job, and water that has already reached the floor moves to restoration after a water loss.

Within about ten miles of 33408, the neighboring ZIPs 33403, 33418, 33477, 33458, and 33404 get the same slab leak and drain response from the same line, so a call from the Lake Park side of Northlake Blvd is handled the same way.
