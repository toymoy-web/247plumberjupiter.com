Slab Leak Repair Cost in Florida: What Sets the Price

No honest single number fits every house. This guide shows the parts of a slab leak bill and the questions that make quotes comparable.

    Short Answer

    Why There Is No Single Slab Leak Price

    Slab leak cost depends on where the leak is, how it is reached, what sits on top of it, and what you choose to do about the pipe. A leak found in an open hallway under tile is a different job from one under a kitchen island. Treat any number quoted before the leak is pinpointed as a guess.

    Published ranges show the problem. One South Florida company's two cost guides list a spot repair through the slab at $1,100 to $3,500 in one and $500 to $2,500 or more in the other, and a third guide lists leak detection alone at $200 to $650. The numbers do not agree because the scope behind them differs.

    Options

    Spot Repair, Reroute, or Repipe

    Spot repair

    • Fixes one leak by opening the slab at that point
    • Lowest cost when the rest of the pipe is sound
    • Another leak may follow in old pipe
    • Needs concrete and floor restoration

    Reroute or repipe

    • Runs new pipe through the attic or walls
    • Avoids breaking more concrete
    • Higher upfront cost
    • Better when leaks keep returning

    Local Context

    Slabs, Age, and Permits in Jupiter

    Most South Florida homes sit on slabs, and Jupiter's housing stock grew fast between 1970 and 1990, when its population rose from 3,136 to 24,986. Older copper and polybutylene supply lines are common in that era. A slab leak in a house that old is often a sign of aging pipe, not a one-off.

    The Town of Jupiter Building Department at 210 Military Trail handles permits inside town limits. Work follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and Florida law requires a licensed contractor for plumbing work. Ask who is pulling the permit and confirm the license before signing.

    Reading a Quote

    How to Read a Written Quote

    A useful quote lists what is included in plain terms. Look for a line for detection or pinpointing, a line for access and concrete work, the pipe repair, restoration of the floor, and the permit. A single number with no breakdown makes comparison impossible.

    Watch for items that appear later. Extra charges for concrete cutting, flooring, or a second leak found during the repair are common surprises, so ask what happens if more is found and how that is priced.

    Total Cost

    When the Cheapest Option Costs More

    A cheap spot repair on old pipe can be the right choice if the rest of the line is sound. If it is not, a second leak may follow within months, with another round of detection, concrete work, and restoration. The water running in the meantime adds to the bill, and so does damage to flooring.

    A reroute or repipe costs more upfront but may cost less over time when the pipe is clearly failing. The deciding evidence is the condition of the remaining pipe, the age of the house, and how many leaks have already occurred.

    Questions

    Five Questions Worth Asking Every Company

    Ask how they pinpoint the leak and whether the pinpoint fee is credited to the repair. Ask whether the quote covers restoring the floor and the concrete. Ask whether a permit is needed and who pulls it. Ask for the warranty terms in writing. Ask what happens if a second leak turns up.

    A company that answers clearly, in writing, before work starts is usually easier to deal with when something unexpected comes up.

    Checklist

    A Quick Comparison Checklist

    When two quotes arrive, line them up side by side. Mark which one includes pinpointing, which one includes concrete and floor work, which one includes the permit, and which lists exclusions. Mark the warranty length on each.

    If one quote is far lower than the others, find out what is missing before you decide. The cheapest quote often leaves out the part that costs the most later.

    Insurance

    Ask About Coverage Before You Open the Floor

    Coverage for slab leaks varies widely. Some policies pay for the access cost to reach a covered leak and others exclude water that escaped over time. Ask your insurer before choosing the repair, and photograph the leak area first.
