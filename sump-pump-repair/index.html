An Honest Answer About Sump Pumps in Jupiter

Basements are exceptionally rare in Florida because of the shallow water table, limestone, and sandy soil. Most Jupiter homes sit on a concrete slab, so the classic basement sump pump you may have seen elsewhere is uncommon here.

Pumps still turn up in specific places. Below-grade garages, elevator pits, pool equipment pits, lower-level rooms, and the plumbing of condo and commercial buildings can all use one. If you have a pump and it has stopped doing its job, this page explains what is likely wrong and what the repair involves.

Warning Signs

Signs a Pump Is Failing

Runs nonstop

A stuck float or a failed check valve keeps the motor cycling.

Will not start

A tripped breaker, a dead float switch, or a burnt motor can all do it.

Loud grinding or rattling

A damaged impeller or a stone in the housing makes it noisy.

Water rising in the pit

The pump may be running but not moving enough water.

Alarm sounding

A high-water alarm means the pit is filling faster than it empties.

Sewage smell near the pit

An ejector with a failed seal or cover can leak odor.

Overview

What a Pump Repair Usually Involves

Most repairs come down to a handful of parts. The float switch tells the pump when to run, the check valve keeps water from flowing back into the pit, and the impeller does the moving. A float that sticks, a valve that fails, or an impeller clogged with debris explains most service calls.

A pump that has run for roughly ten years with maintenance is nearing the end of its useful life. A repair on an older pump may only buy time, so the choice is explained before any work begins.

Repair or Replace

When to Repair and When to Replace

Repair is usually right when

  • The pump is well short of its expected life
  • The problem is a float, a check valve, or a tripped breaker
  • The motor and impeller turn freely
  • There was one failure and not a series

Replace is usually right when

  • The pump is close to ten years old
  • The motor is burnt or the housing is cracked
  • Repairs keep coming back
  • The pit needs a larger or backup-equipped pump

Jupiter Conditions

Where Pumps Turn Up Around Jupiter

Condo and commercial buildings with below-grade garages, loading areas, or elevator pits may use sump pumps, and larger buildings may use sewage ejectors or a duplex system. Pool equipment pits and lower-level rooms in some newer luxury homes can also have a pump. Which of these applies depends on the building, so say what you have when you call.

Homes on septic systems, common in Jupiter Farms and parts of Hobe Sound, can have an effluent pump between the tank and the drain field. That is a septic contractor's trade, and the septic pumping page explains how those calls are handled. A large HOA or municipal lift station is also outside the scope of a typical plumbing repair.

Discharge matters too. Clear water from a sump pump is generally not allowed to go into the sanitary sewer, so the discharge line should run to a permitted outdoor location. A technician checks where yours ends before replacing the pump. The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), covers sumps and ejectors in Chapter 7, and permit needs depend on the work and the address.

Prep

What to Do If the Pump Fails Tonight

Cut the power

Turn off the breaker for the pump before touching the pit or the cord.

Keep clear of the water

Water near a pit with an energized cord is a shock risk.

Move items up

Lift boxes, electronics, and stored items off the floor near the pit.

Check the alarm and breaker

A tripped GFCI is the simplest fix. Reset it once and note whether it trips again.
