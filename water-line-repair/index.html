Wet Spot in the Yard or a Meter That Will Not Stop

A soggy patch of lawn with no rain, a meter that keeps moving after you close the main at the house, or a sudden drop in pressure are the usual signs of a leak in the service line. That is the pipe that carries water from the meter to the house.

It is a different job from a leak inside the house. The line may run underground for fifty feet or more, under grass, driveway, or landscaping.

Emergency

Emergency Water Line Break: Wet Yard or No Water at the House

Water bubbling out of the ground, a swampy patch in the yard, or sudden loss of water at every tap usually points to the line between the meter and the house. If the leak is ahead of the meter, it belongs to the utility.

Close the house main and check the meter. If it still moves, the loss is outside. Call the 24/7 line and tell dispatch where the water is surfacing.

Overview

What Water Line Work Covers

Water line repair and replacement covers the service line on the property, from the meter or curb stop to the house. A plumber locates the leak, then repairs a short section or replaces the whole run. Methods include digging a trench, boring under a driveway, or pulling a new line through the old path.

The pipe may be copper, polyethylene, PVC, or galvanized steel depending on age. Replacement usually uses polyethylene or copper, installed to the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Jupiter Conditions

The Town's Water System and Your Side of the Meter

The Town of Jupiter Utilities Department delivers water to a service area with more than 30,000 connections and about 94,000 residents. As part of the EPA's Lead and Copper Rule revisions, it completed a service line inventory and reports that the system is lead free. It publishes an interactive map and says homes built after 1976 are highly unlikely to have a lead service line.

That is useful context, but it covers the Town's inventory of lines to the meter. The line on your property is still your responsibility, and its age and material vary. A galvanized line that is 40 years old behaves very differently from a newer polyethylene one.

Tequesta runs its own village water system, so a leak on the street side there is a call to the village. In Jupiter Farms there is usually no service line at all, because most homes use private wells.

Low Water Pressure

Low Water Pressure at Every Tap

Low pressure at every fixture usually points to something between the meter and the house. Common causes include a partly closed valve, a failing pressure regulator, a leak in the service line, or old galvanized pipe that has narrowed inside.

Pressure that is low at one fixture is a fixture problem. Low at all of them is a line or regulator problem. Tell dispatch which you have and whether the meter moves with taps off.

Meter to House and Well to House

Leaks Between the Meter or the Well and the House

On city water, the line from the meter box to the house is usually the property owner's responsibility, and the utility can confirm where its side ends. On a well, the pipe from the wellhouse to the home can run a long way underground. A wet strip of lawn, unusually green grass above the line, or a pump that never rests are the clues.

Pressure testing and locating the line come before digging. Replacing the full run is sometimes a better choice than patching an old line.

Around Jupiter

Service Line Repair From Tequesta Park to Jupiter Farms

Tequesta Park, also known as Kiwanis Recreation Park, holds the village's water wells, and the village runs its own water utility. The village owns the street side and the meter, and the line after the meter is a plumbing job.

In Jupiter proper the Town of Jupiter Utilities Department supplies water. In Jupiter Farms most homes use private wells instead, so the line to the house starts at a pressure tank and not at a meter.
