Third Leak This Year?

A homeowner who has repaired a pinhole leak, then a second, then a third is usually looking at the same underlying problem: pipe that is at the end of its life. Each repair fixes one spot and the next weak spot fails soon after.

That is the situation repiping is meant for. Instead of chasing leaks, the old supply system is replaced as a whole.

Overview

What Repiping Means

Repiping replaces the water supply lines in a house, from the main shutoff to each fixture, with new pipe. Drain and sewer lines are separate and not part of a standard repipe. The work involves opening walls or ceilings in selected spots, running new pipe, connecting fixtures, and patching.

Common replacement materials are PEX, a flexible plastic pipe, and copper. PEX is faster to install and resists freezing damage and some corrosion. Copper is traditional, durable, and has a long record.

Jupiter Conditions

Jupiter's Housing and Its Pipes

U.S. Census counts show Jupiter growing from 3,136 residents in 1970 to 9,868 in 1980 and 24,986 in 1990. Polybutylene was widely installed in the United States from about 1978 to 1995, so a significant share of Jupiter's older houses may have it behind the walls.

Slab construction complicates the job. Many supply lines run through the attic or under the slab, so repiping often reroutes supply through the attic and walls and abandons the old lines in place.

The Town of Jupiter's water service lines, which run from the main to the meter, are not part of a house repipe. The Town reports that its system has no known lead service lines, so a repipe concerns the pipes on your side of the meter.

Decision

PEX or Copper

PEX

  • Faster to install with fewer walls opened
  • Flexible, so fewer joints
  • Resists scale and some corrosion
  • Needs UV protection and good fittings

Copper

  • Long record, often 50 years or more
  • Rigid and fire-resistant
  • More joints and more labor
  • Can suffer pinhole leaks in aggressive water
Process

What a Repipe Involves

1

Survey

A plumber walks the house and plans the route, the number of fixtures, and wall access.

2

Permit

The permit is pulled with the Town or county before work starts.

3

Rough in

New lines are run to each fixture and the old lines are capped.

4

Pressure-test

The new system is pressurized and checked.

5

Inspect and patch

The inspector signs off, then holes are patched and the water is turned on.
