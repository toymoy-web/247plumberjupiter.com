Seasonal

Salt Air, Storm Season, and Juno Beach Plumbing

Hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30, and Juno Beach has seen what a late-season storm can do. The original pier was destroyed in a November 1984 storm, and a new one of nearly 1,000 feet opened in 1999. Plumbing on the barrier island faces the same salt, wind, and humidity as the pier does.

Exposed brass and chrome fittings corrode faster in salt air, particularly hose bibs, rooftop and balcony valves, and the shutoff valves in ground-floor utility closets. A fitting that looks fine in October can seize or leak after a wet summer.

The emergency line is staffed around the clock all year, and storm weeks are when calls spike.

Warning Signs

Signs a Coastal Plumbing Problem Is Brewing

Green or white crust

Mineral or corrosion buildup on a fitting means moisture is working on the metal.

Rust streaks below a valve

A slow drip that dries between visits leaves a stain before it leaves a puddle.

A stuck shutoff valve

A valve that will not turn when you test it may fail when you need it.

Hissing in a quiet unit

Pressurized water escaping inside a wall or ceiling often makes a faint sound.

Damp garage ceiling below a unit

Water traveling along a slab edge or stack often appears in the unit below first.

Drains gurgling together

Several fixtures complaining at once points at the stack, not one trap.

Emergency

Emergency Plumber Juno Beach FL 33408: Calling From a Condo or a House

At the beach, the first minutes of an emergency often decide whether a leak stays in one unit or crosses three floors. When you call the 24/7 line from 33408, say if you are in an oceanfront condo, a townhouse, or a house west of US 1.

Condo owners should keep the building's after-hours number handy, since the association often controls the riser shutoff and may need to send maintenance first. House owners can usually act alone by closing the main, cutting power to wet areas, and waiting for the crew.

Dispatch also asks which utility bills the address, because Juno Beach is served by the Town of Jupiter Utilities in some places and Seacoast Utility Authority in others. That answer tells the crew where the public side ends.

Neighborhood

Streets Named for Planets: 1950s Lots and 1960s Condos

Juno Beach was platted in 1948 and incorporated in 1953, when it had about 130 year-round residents and 1,500 in season. In 1958 the Juno Beach Garden Club named the streets south of Donald Ross Road between US 1 and A1A for planets and Roman gods, which is why Juno Beach has a Mars Way and a Saturn Lane.

Condominium growth followed in the 1960s along Ocean Drive. That gives the town two housing types with two plumbing problems. The 1950s lots tend to have older drain and supply lines under the slab. The condo buildings have stacks and risers shared by many units, and work on them is governed by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Utilities

Which Utility Serves Your Address

Juno Beach does not sit cleanly inside one water service area. The Town of Jupiter Utilities Department says its system serves Juno Beach, and Seacoast Utility Authority also lists Juno Beach among the communities it serves. Your bill tells you which agency applies to your address.

Sewer service is split too, since the Loxahatchee River District serves parts of Juno Beach. That matters in an emergency. A pressure drop that hits the whole block is a call to your utility, while a leak after the meter is a plumbing call.

Landmarks

Loggerhead Marinelife Center, the Pier, and Seminole Golf Club

Loggerhead Marinelife Center at 14200 US 1 is the town's best-known landmark, and Loggerhead Park and Juno Dunes Natural Area sit along the same stretch. The Juno Beach Pier, built in 1999, extends nearly 1,000 feet into the ocean.

Seminole Golf Club, a Donald Ross course from 1929, is also in town. Those landmarks help dispatch place a caller who is not sure of the street.
