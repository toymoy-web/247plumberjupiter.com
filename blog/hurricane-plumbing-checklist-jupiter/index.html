Hurricane Plumbing Checklist for Jupiter, FL Homes

A short, practical list for the plumbing side of storm prep: what to do before the storm, during a watch, and once the water comes back.

By the Jupiter 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 3, 2026 · Updated October 3, 2026

    Short Answer

    The Five Things That Matter Most

    Know where your main water shutoff is and make sure it turns. Shut it off if you evacuate. Do not run water or flush toilets if sewage could back up. After the storm, check for leaks when pressure returns, and follow any boil-water notice exactly.

    That covers most of the plumbing risk in a hurricane. The rest of this list adds the details for Jupiter's two utilities, septic homes, and the water heater.

    Local Note

    Jupiter Note: Check Your Evacuation Zone Each Year

    Palm Beach County runs an evacuation zone lookup called Know Your Zone. A Jupiter-area town hurricane workbook tells residents to check their zone by address each year, since zones should not be assumed to match last year's. It also says shelters are announced close to the event and that bridge limits and traffic can make a late evacuation dangerous.

    For plumbing, the same workbook suggests filling bathtubs and containers with water for flushing and cleaning, and knowing how to shut off water if told to. Residents of Hobe Sound are in Martin County and should use that county's alerts. Juno Beach and North Palm Beach residents use the Palm Beach County tools.

    Source: Jupiter Inlet Colony 2026 Hurricane Ready Workbook, which lists the Palm Beach County lookup.

    Local History

    What Jupiter's Storm History Teaches

    Hurricane Frances passed in September 2004 with an estimated peak gust of 91 mph at Jupiter Inlet, according to the National Weather Service. Jeanne followed on September 26 and brought locally severe rainfall flooding to Jupiter and Palm Beach Gardens. Both made landfall on Hutchinson Island, about two miles apart.

    The plumbing lessons were practical. Wind and falling trees strained buried lines, rain overloaded drains, and water quality notices followed damaged mains. A house that had been checked before the storm handled it better than one with an old valve and a bad fitting.

    Prep

    Water, Sewer, and Septic Tips

    Know your utility

    Jupiter water comes from the Town, and sewer comes from the Loxahatchee River District. Septic homes have neither.

    Limit use in heavy rain

    Avoid long showers and laundry when drains are overloaded.

    Septic homes

    Switch off the well pump breaker if you evacuate and avoid heavy water use when the ground is saturated.

    Watch the low drains

    A gurgling floor drain or toilet signals a stressed line, so stop running water.

    After the Storm

    Boil-Water Notices and Pressure Surges

    The Town of Jupiter posts boil-water notice information on its utilities site. If a notice is issued, follow it before drinking, cooking, brushing teeth, or making ice. Private wells are not covered by Town notices, so owners should consider testing the well after flooding.

    When service returns, pressure can surge through lines weakened by wind or age. Open a few taps, check under sinks and around the water heater, and watch the meter with everything off. A moving meter means a new leak.

    Where to Go Next

    If Something Failed

    A split pipe falls under repair of a failed supply line, and a damaged tank is a tank repair call. Water that has already spread is covered under water mitigation, which starts drying in the first hours.

    On the Martin County side, the Hobe Sound page explains how utilities and permits differ.

    Water Heater

    The Water Heater Before and After

    If you evacuate, consider switching off power or gas to the water heater and closing its cold-water supply. A full tank holds a few dozen gallons, and some utilities and plumbers suggest it as an emergency supply of non-drinking water if the house is occupied and the tank is intact.

    After the storm, check the area around the heater for dampness, rust, and a dripping relief valve. A heater that was fine before a power surge can fail afterward, and the heater replacement page explains the permit and sizing steps.

    Outdoors

    Hose Bibs, Irrigation, and the Pool

    Turn off and drain accessible outdoor spigots and irrigation valves before a storm, and shut off pool fill lines. Flooded yards can submerge backflow devices, so ask a tester whether an irrigation device needs a check after a flood, and keep up with any notice from the Town.

    Debris in the yard can also break exposed pipes and fittings. Walk the property after the storm and look for wet spots, which can mean a broken line below ground.

    Gas and Generators

    Gas Lines and Standby Generators

    If you shut off the gas before the storm, do not turn it back on yourself. Have the utility or a qualified contractor do it after inspecting the system. If you smell gas at any time, leave and call 911.

    Standby generators draw a lot of gas, and a poorly sized line is a common problem. The gas line installation and repair page explains sizing, permits, and testing.

    Small Habits

    Small Habits That Help Every Season

    Keep a flashlight and a basin wrench near the main shutoff, and label the valve with a bright tag so anyone in the house can find it. Walk the house once a year with a notepad and list every shutoff under sinks and behind toilets, since stuck ones are common after years of disuse.

    If you travel often, a water shutoff valve with leak sensors can alert you to a problem while you are away. It is an add-on, not a requirement, and it does not replace knowing where the manual shutoff is.

    Insurance

    Document and Report

    Take dated photos of damage and the source before cleanup. Under Florida Statutes 627.70132, a policyholder has one year to give notice of a new property claim, so report early. Standard homeowners policies generally do not cover flood, so check if you carry a separate flood policy.
